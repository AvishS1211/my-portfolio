01Overview

Students preparing for study-abroad visa interviews (F1, J1, B1, B2 for the US, plus France and UK equivalents) had one real prep option: paying ₹2,000–₹10,000 per in-person mock interview. Since one session is rarely enough to actually prepare, this became an expensive, repeat cost most students couldn't justify.

VisaMonk turned mock interview prep into an affordable, AI-driven product students could practise with as many times as they needed, and packaged it as a B2B offering banks and education-finance partners could bundle for their customers.

02The problem

Cost was prohibitive for repeat practice

A single in-person mock interview cost ₹2K–₹10K, and one round is almost never enough to walk into a real visa interview prepared.

Prep was inconsistent

Quality depended entirely on who was running the mock session; there was no standardised, scalable way to prepare students across visa types and countries.

No single tool covered every visa type

Students applying for F1, J1, B1, B2 (US) or visas for France and the UK each needed different question banks and interview formats, with no unified prep experience.

Goal

Make realistic, repeatable visa interview practice affordable and available on demand, and package it as something institutional partners would pay to offer their students.

03Process

The hardest part of this project wasn't the interface; it was building a credible, realistic question and evaluation base for an AI to run mock interviews against.

  1. Sourced real interview data at scale

    We scraped past interview experiences, both successful and rejected, from Facebook groups, Telegram communities and other student forums, building a set of roughly 1,000 real questions with the actual answers candidates gave.

  2. Repeated this per visa type and country

    The same sourcing and structuring process was run separately for F1, J1, B1, B2 (US), and for France and UK visa interviews, since each has a distinct question style and evaluation criteria.

  3. Partnered with Remasto

    An AI interview company with a strong reputation at the time. We handed off the structured question-and-answer dataset for model training, so the AI could run and evaluate mock interviews realistically.

  4. Designed the product around a widget, not a custom AI interface

    Since Remasto powered the actual interview experience, the design work focused on the surrounding product: onboarding, pricing, and getting a student from "I need to prepare" to "I'm in a mock interview" with as little friction as possible.

04Solution

The product was intentionally simple: the complexity lived in the data and AI model, not the UI.

05Results

06Learnings

Data quality is the product, even when you're "just" doing design

The month spent scraping and structuring real interview data mattered more to the outcome than any screen; worth remembering when design work looks thin on a project like this.

B2B repositioning changes the design job

Once this became a partner-bundled product rather than a direct-to-consumer one, the design priority shifted from consumer conversion to making the pricing and onboarding legible for partners reselling it.

A widget-based build is still a full design problem

Even without owning the core interview UI, there was real work in making the surrounding experience (onboarding, pricing, expectation-setting) feel like one coherent product rather than a page bolted onto a third-party tool.
